# Discovery Call Script — 30-minute audit kickoff

**Purpose:** Convert an inbound audit request into a productive 5-day audit and (eventually) a hybrid engagement.

**Length:** 30 minutes. Hard stop.

**Outcome:** (a) Firm-specific audit kickoff with priority queries and peer set defined; (b) trust established; (c) clear path to the post-audit walkthrough call.

---

## Pre-call (10 minutes prep)

- [ ] Re-read the audit form submission
- [ ] Visit firm's website — note: practice areas, partners, recent news, blog cadence
- [ ] Run **2 quick AI tests** before the call: type firm name into ChatGPT and Perplexity. Note results.
- [ ] Pull up prior emails/intros for context

## Opening (3 min)

> "Thanks for taking the time. I'll keep this to 30 minutes — that's enough to get me what I need to deliver a useful audit on a 5-day turnaround. Before we start: did you have specific questions about how the audit works, or should I start with my discovery questions?"

[Answer any open questions briefly. Then proceed.]

## Discovery (15 min)

### About the firm

1. *"Tell me how you describe what your firm does — in one sentence."*
2. *"Who's your typical client? Industry, size, what they hire you for?"*
3. *"What practice areas / service lines do you most want to win new business in?"*
4. *"What's the firm's current marketing setup? Anything in retainer with anyone?"*

### About the trigger

5. *"What made you fill out the audit form now? Was there a specific moment?"*

[**Listen carefully here.** This answer is the most important data point of the entire call. The trigger reveals what the firm cares about.]

6. *"Have you typed your firm name into ChatGPT or Perplexity recently? What did you see?"*

[If they haven't done this, walk through what *you* found in the pre-call. Don't be theatrical about it — present it as one data point, not a sales hook.]

### About priority queries

7. *"If a prospect were going to ChatGPT and asking about hiring someone like your firm — what would they type?"*

[Capture 5 priority queries. Examples for law: "Who's a good business litigation attorney in Providence?"; "How does breach of contract litigation work in Rhode Island?"; "What's the cost of commercial litigation in RI?"]

### About peer set

8. *"Who are the 3–5 firms you most often compete against — the ones you'd genuinely consider peers?"*

[Capture firm names. We'll run the same priority queries against them in the audit.]

### About decision dynamics

9. *"If we did this audit and the findings made a strong case for ongoing work — who else would weigh in on that decision?"*

[This is the multi-stakeholder map. Solo decision-makers get a 5-day audit; multi-stakeholder firms get a 5-day audit + a deck designed for partner review.]

10. *"What's your timeline? Is this exploratory, or are you trying to address something by [next quarter]?"*

## Logistics (7 min)

- [ ] Confirm audit scope: 5 priority queries × 5 AI engines + schema audit + content scoring + competitive map + 30/60/90 roadmap
- [ ] Confirm delivery: written PDF in 5 business days from today
- [ ] Schedule the walkthrough call (60 minutes) for 5 business days from now + 1 day buffer
- [ ] Confirm pricing: $1,500 — *or* free for the founding-client tier ("you're firm number [N] of 25")
- [ ] Confirm point of contact for any audit questions during the 5-day delivery window

## Close (5 min)

> *"Two things I want to be honest about before we end. First, I'm not going to try to sell you on next steps before the audit lands — the audit either makes a clear case or it doesn't, and either outcome is fine with me. Second, the audit findings are yours regardless of whether we work together afterward. If you take it to a different agency, that's a perfectly legitimate use.*
>
> *That said — most firms that take the audit do end up working with us, because the findings tend to point to the same playbook: schema, content restructuring, vertical-specific publication. If you're interested in seeing what the engagement would look like, I can prep that in parallel and walk you through it on the same call.*
>
> *Sound good? Anything else before we end?"*

## Post-call (10 min)

- [ ] Send confirmation email within 30 minutes (template below)
- [ ] Add to CRM: firm, contact, vertical, source, audit ID, delivery date, walkthrough date
- [ ] Log priority queries, peer set, trigger, decision dynamics
- [ ] If multi-stakeholder, prep the partner-review one-pager during the audit week
- [ ] Block calendar for actual audit production: ~12 hours of deep work over 4–5 days

## Things to listen for during the call

**Strong-buyer signals:**
- They've already noticed competitors getting cited
- They have a marketing budget already allocated; this is a shift, not new spend
- They name a specific deal lost or trigger event
- They mention "our partners" or "the committee" in a way that suggests organized decision-making
- They ask follow-up questions about methodology rather than skepticism questions about whether GEO works

**Weaker-buyer signals (consider whether to proceed with audit):**
- They want a 30-day "trial" before paying anything
- They expect "results" within 7 days
- They have no marketing budget allocated and ask about payment plans for the audit
- They describe their firm in superlatives without specifics
- They want SEO + GEO + paid ads + social + PR all bundled

**Walk-away signals:**
- They're outside our 5 verticals
- They're sub-$500K revenue (audit value won't justify their expected close rate)
- They want fake reviews, fake testimonials, or anything outside compliance norms
- They're rude or dismissive on the kickoff call

It is fine to politely conclude the call with *"I don't think we're the right fit — let me give you a couple of names of agencies that might be."* Better than a bad fit.

## Conversion benchmark

Across all founding-client audits, internal target:
- **Audit-to-walkthrough-call kept:** 95%
- **Walkthrough-to-proposal:** 70%
- **Proposal-to-signed-engagement:** 45%
- **End-to-end (audit-to-signed):** ~30%

Track these per-audit in the CRM. Adjust the script and the audit deliverable based on what's actually converting.
