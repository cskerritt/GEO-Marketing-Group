The shift from Google to AI is no longer a "future of search" trend. **73% of B2B buyers now use AI tools in their research process. 37% consult AI before Google. 89% consider AI search a top source throughout the buying process.** [^1] [^2]

These are not projections. These are 2025–2026 data points from buyer surveys and traffic analytics. The shift is already happening. Most professional services firms are not yet adjusting.

This article walks through the numbers, what they mean for firms whose pipeline depends on inbound research traffic, and what to do about it.

## The headline numbers

| Metric | Value | Source |
|---|---|---|
| B2B buyers using AI tools in research | **73%** | Bain analysis (2025) |
| B2B buyers consulting AI before Google | **37%** | Metricus (2026) |

| Domains cited by both ChatGPT and Perplexity | **Only 11%** | upGrowth (2026) |

The conversion rate is the sleeper stat. **AI search traffic converts five times better than Google organic.** Buyers who arrive at your site via an AI link have already done their evaluation work — they've asked the AI an open-ended question, weighed the AI's synthesized response, and chosen to click your firm's name out of a small set. They are not casual researchers; they are pre-qualified buyers.

For a professional services firm, this is the most consequential number in the table. A 14.2% conversion rate is closer to "qualified referral" than "cold inbound."

## Why this is happening so fast

Three reinforcing dynamics:

**1. The AI engines now provide citations.** When ChatGPT, Claude, Perplexity, and Google AI Overviews provide cited responses with source links, they replace the function of Google's blue links: *finding sources for the user's question*. The user doesn't need to click through to a search result and evaluate ten options anymore — the AI has done that synthesis.

**2. The user experience is genuinely better for research-heavy queries.** A B2B buyer evaluating tax-strategy approaches, choosing a litigation firm, or scoping an A&E project gets more usable answers from an AI conversation than from a Google search results page. The AI synthesizes, contextualizes, and answers follow-up questions in the same thread. Google requires the user to do the synthesis themselves across ten tabs.

**3. The behavior compounds.** Once a buyer has used AI for one research-heavy purchase decision and gotten a useful answer, they default to AI for the next one. The 37% who consult AI before Google today are tomorrow's 50%, then 70%.

## The asymmetry that's the real opportunity

**Only 11% of domains cited by ChatGPT are also cited by Perplexity.** This is not a small number. It is a fundamental difference in what each engine retrieves and rewards.

For a firm investing in GEO, this means:

- You can win ChatGPT citations and still lose Perplexity citations
- You can dominate Google rankings and be invisible to both AI engines
- Each engine requires explicit attention to its source-selection biases

ChatGPT favors Wikipedia-style authoritative content (47.9% of top citations). [^4] Perplexity favors Reddit and recent discussion (46.7% Reddit). Google AI Overviews favor multi-modal content including YouTube (23.3%). Claude prizes precision and verifiable accuracy.

A real GEO program optimizes per-engine. It doesn't try to win all of them with one tactic — that doesn't work — but instead allocates effort by engine relevance to the firm's audience.

## What this means for professional services firms specifically

Three implications:

**1. The pipeline impact is already measurable.** Firms with active web analytics are seeing AI-attributable traffic land in GA4 (it shows up as referrer-less direct or as `chatgpt.com`, `perplexity.ai`, `claude.ai`, etc.). The volume is small but the conversion rate is high. The volume will grow.

**2. The traditional SEO pipeline is shrinking.** Not because Google is failing, but because the *upstream* of Google searches is shifting. Buyers who used to search Google now ask AI. The Google traffic was a derivative of buyer research behavior; that behavior has changed.

**3. The brands that get cited now will compound.** AI engines have memory in two senses: short-term (current crawl indexes) and long-term (training data). Firms that establish citations now get included in future training cycles, which gives them durable citation positioning even as the engines evolve.

## What to do this quarter

For a firm not yet doing GEO, three concrete moves:

**1. Take a baseline reading.** Type your firm name and your top five practice-area / service queries into ChatGPT, Claude, Perplexity, and Google AI Overviews. Note who shows up. This is your before picture.

**2. Add FAQ schema to your top 5 pages.** This is the single highest-ROI structural change. FAQ schema correlates with disproportionate citation lift in B2B verticals. A competent web developer can do it in a day; the impact is durable.

**3. Restructure your homepage and top service page so the first 200 words complete the answer to the page's primary query.** The Aggarwal et al. paper documented 20–40% relative lift from this single change. [^3]

These three moves cost little and start the citation flywheel. Sustained GEO work is more involved (full schema deployment, content publishing cadence, authority building, monitoring), but those three changes are within reach for any firm willing to do them.

## What's coming next

Reasonable expectations for the next 24 months:

- **2026:** AI search captures 25–40% of B2B research-stage queries (vs. ~10% in 2024). GEO becomes a line item in mid-market marketing budgets.
- **2027:** AI search exceeds 50% of research-stage queries for high-consideration B2B. Agentic AI buyers (autonomous purchasing assistants) emerge as a real factor. GEO terminology likely standardizes.
- **2028:** "AI-first" replaces "mobile-first" as the dominant content design paradigm.

The firms that act in 2026 establish citation moats that compound. The firms that wait until 2028 will be playing catch-up against competitors whose authority signals have already been baked into AI training cycles.
