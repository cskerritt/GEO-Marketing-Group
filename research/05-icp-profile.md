# Ideal Customer Profile (ICP) — GEO

**Research date:** 2026-04-25
**Purpose:** Define exactly who GEO sells to. Five vertical-specific personas, each with pains, triggers, decision criteria, objections, and the matched service tier. This document drives messaging, sales scripts, and the website's industry pages.

---

## 1. ICP Definition

**Primary ICP:** Owner, principal, or marketing director at a Rhode Island, Southeast Massachusetts, or (Y2+) Northeast US **professional services firm**, $1M–$25M revenue, 5–75 employees, in one of five verticals:

1. **Law firms** (boutique to mid-size)
2. **CPA / accounting practices** (multi-partner)
3. **Consulting firms** (management, strategy, IT consulting)
4. **Financial advisors / wealth management RIAs**
5. **Architecture & engineering (A&E) firms**

**Excluded from ICP:**
- Solo practitioners under $500K revenue (budget too thin, sale cycle too long)
- BigLaw / Big Four / Big Engineering (sales cycle 6+ months, marketing buy is corporate)
- Pure B2C professional services (medical/dental, real estate brokerages, salons) — different buying behavior, different content needs
- Tech startups / SaaS — well-served by First Page Sage / Discovered Labs / TripleDart; not our wheelhouse

## 2. The Five Personas

Each persona = the actual buyer at a typical ICP firm. Names are illustrative composites built from market research.

---

### PERSONA A — *Patrick the Managing Partner* (Law)

**Firm shape:** 8-attorney boutique law firm, Providence, RI. Practice areas: business law + estates + commercial litigation. ~$3.5M revenue. 12 total staff.

**About Patrick:**
- 56 years old, RWU Law alum, partner since 38, managing partner for the last 7 years
- Owns 35% of the firm's equity; effectively controls partner decisions on operations
- Has used ChatGPT for personal research; suspicious of "AI-generated content" but curious
- Reads Rhode Island Lawyers Weekly cover-to-cover; attends 2–3 RIBA events/year
- Drives 25 minutes from East Greenwich to the office most days

**Marketing budget:** ~$60,000/yr split between website maintenance ($8K), GBP/local SEO ($18K), Chamber/RIBA sponsorships ($15K), Yellow Pages-type directory ads (still!) ($6K), Bar Journal ads ($8K), and one annual print campaign ($5K)

**Pains:**
- Two associates have left in 18 months citing "the firm doesn't show up online" as a reason; this stings
- Patrick's own daughter (Brown undergrad) asked ChatGPT for a "good business attorney in RI" and the firm wasn't named; competing firms were
- Referrals are slowing — formerly 60% of new client work, now 40%
- Younger partners (the 38–45 cohort) keep proposing "AI things" that he doesn't understand; he wants to be perceived as forward, not stuck

**Triggers (what makes him buy now):**
- A peer mention at RI Bar event ("we just hired this firm called GEO and our citation rates 3x'd")
- A new associate during interview asks "how does the firm think about AI search?"
- A partner meeting in which someone displays a ChatGPT result that excludes their firm

**Decision criteria:**
- Is the agency *credible to other RI lawyers*? (Local references > national reputation)
- Is the deliverable specific? (He doesn't buy "ongoing services" without a defined artifact)
- Will it embarrass him if it doesn't work? (He fears looking foolish)
- Cost: $20K–$45K/yr range is acceptable; over $50K/yr requires a partner vote

**Objections:**
- *"Isn't this just SEO with a new name?"* → Show citation patterns; demonstrate Perplexity/ChatGPT differ from Google
- *"Can't my paralegal do this?"* → Yes, badly; show a 4-page sample of a real GEO build vs. a generic content task
- *"Compliance — what if AI cites us with a misleading claim?"* → Show the citation review/correction layer in the retainer
- *"Six other agencies have pitched me; what makes you different?"* → Vertical depth ("we only do this for law/CPA/consulting/financial/A&E"); RI presence

**Matched service:** **Audit ($1,500) → Build ($7,500) → GEO Growth retainer ($2,500/mo)**.
**Annual value:** $39,000.
**Sales cycle:** 6–10 weeks.
**Sales notes:** In-person meeting required. Bring a printed audit deliverable. Mention RIBA, his peer firms (without naming clients), his concern about associate retention.

### PERSONA B — *Diane the Marketing Director* (CPA)

**Firm shape:** 12-partner, 60-person mid-size CPA firm, Warwick RI. Tax + audit + outsourced accounting + advisory. ~$15M revenue.

**About Diane:**
- 42 years old, marketing director for 6 years (promoted internally from BD coordinator)
- BS in business; MBA from Bryant
- Champions modernization at the firm — she introduced HubSpot, LinkedIn outreach, and the firm podcast
- Has executive sponsorship from one of the younger managing partners; slight friction with the senior tax partners who think marketing is "fluffy"
- Personally uses ChatGPT for outline drafting, content ideas, research

**Marketing budget:** ~$280,000/yr — website ($25K), HubSpot ($28K), digital ads ($60K), events/sponsorships ($45K), content production ($55K), PR retainer ($35K), other ($32K)

**Pains:**
- Lead volume from organic search dropped 18% YoY; she suspects AI search is the cause but can't *prove* it to the partners
- The firm's content (white papers, articles) gets posted but doesn't generate citations or backlinks
- Internal pressure to show ROI on her budget; partners are renegotiating headcount
- She knows competing CPA firms (KLR, Sansiveri, etc.) are starting to do "AI things" but doesn't have visibility into what

**Triggers:**
- A partner forwards her a competitor's article that "ChatGPT recommended"
- The firm's biggest deal of the quarter mentions in the kickoff that "we found you through Perplexity"
- A peer marketing director at a RISCPA event mentions hiring a GEO agency

**Decision criteria:**
- Will this make her look smart in front of partners?
- Can she explain it in 4 minutes to a 65-year-old tax partner?
- Will it integrate with HubSpot? (She does not want a parallel system)
- ROI: she needs leading indicators within 90 days
- Cost: $30K–$80K/yr is in her budget authority; over $80K requires partner review

**Objections:**
- *"Our compliance and tax content is too technical to optimize for AI"* → Show CPA-specific examples; explain how schema makes technical content *more* citable
- *"We already have an SEO vendor"* → Position as complement, not replacement; offer to coordinate with existing vendor
- *"How do we measure success?"* → Define KPIs upfront: citations per month, AI-referred sessions, AI-attributed leads
- *"Our partners will block this"* → Provide a partner-friendly one-pager + 4-min video pitch she can show internally

**Matched service:** **Audit ($1,500) → Build + Authority ($12,500) → GEO Growth retainer ($2,500/mo) often upgraded to Authority ($4,500/mo) at month 6.**
**Annual value:** $50,000–$70,000.
**Sales cycle:** 8–14 weeks (multi-stakeholder).
**Sales notes:** First call is to Diane alone. She'll line up a partner intro for call 2. Expect to meet 3–5 stakeholders before close. Bring CPA-firm-specific case study materials and a 1-page executive summary designed for printing.

### PERSONA C — *Mark the Consulting Firm Owner*

**Firm shape:** Mark founded a 14-person management consulting practice 8 years ago after 12 years at Bain. Specializes in operations and supply-chain consulting for mid-market manufacturers. Office in Cambridge MA + remote staff in RI/CT. ~$4.5M revenue.

**About Mark:**
- 47 years old, ex-Bain operations associate partner, MBA Wharton
- Highly comfortable with AI tools; uses ChatGPT, Claude, and Perplexity daily for research and synthesis
- Genuinely worried that AI is commoditizing the strategic-thinking work his firm sells
- Reads HBR, Stratechery, MIT Tech Review; listens to Acquired, Lenny's Podcast, others
- Personally writes occasional LinkedIn posts; has 8K followers

**Marketing budget:** Lean — $50,000/yr. Website ($8K), LinkedIn Sales Navigator + ads ($14K), one annual industry conference sponsorship ($10K), content ghostwriter for himself ($14K), other ($4K)

**Pains:**
- Lost a $200K engagement to a competitor whose CEO had "10× the LinkedIn presence"
- Realizes that *his* firm's best work — frameworks, case studies, methodology essays — exists internally but isn't published
- Knows he should publish more but can't make himself write
- Sees AI search as *both* threat (commoditizing) and opportunity (a new distribution channel for his ideas)

**Triggers:**
- A prospect explicitly mentions during a sales call: "I asked ChatGPT for ops consultants and you weren't on the list"
- His best associate gets recruited away by a competitor with better content presence
- He reads an article about GEO and realizes he's late

**Decision criteria:**
- Substance — he can sniff out marketing fluff in 30 seconds
- Founder fit — does the agency "get" professional services work?
- Speed — he wants results in weeks, not quarters
- Personal time investment — he won't spend 5 hours/week on this
- Cost: budget is tight; willing to spend $20K–$30K/yr if value is clear

**Objections:**
- *"Most agencies are bullshit; show me you're not"* → Concrete deliverables, citable case data, refuse to oversell
- *"I don't have time to be the content engine"* → Position GEO Authority retainer as ghostwriting; he reviews drafts, doesn't write them
- *"I can do this myself with ChatGPT"* → Acknowledge yes, technically — but the 60 hours/year of his time at his hourly rate >> our retainer

**Matched service:** **Audit ($1,500) → Foundation ($3,500) → GEO Authority retainer ($4,500/mo)**. The Authority tier (founder ghostwriting) is the unlock — it solves his publish-more problem.
**Annual value:** $59,000.
**Sales cycle:** 4–8 weeks (Mark is decisive once he's bought-in).
**Sales notes:** Skip the printed proposal — send a 3-page memo. He responds to substance, not packaging. Reference Aggarwal et al. by name; he'll respect that we know the academic underpinning.

### PERSONA D — *Jen the RIA Founder* (Financial Advisor)

**Firm shape:** Jen founded a fee-only RIA 11 years ago. 4-advisor team, $230M AUM, primarily multi-generational families and small business owners in RI/MA. Office in Wickford RI. ~$2.4M revenue.

**About Jen:**
- 52 years old, CFP® and CFA®
- Compliance-conscious — every public communication must pass her CCO (a part-time consultant)
- Tech-functional but not tech-forward; uses Wealthbox CRM, Black Diamond reporting
- Active in FPA RI and the broader CFP Board community; speaks at 2–3 events/year
- Reads Citywire, ThinkAdvisor, Investment News

**Marketing budget:** ~$80,000/yr — website ($12K), email marketing tool + content writer ($24K), event sponsorships + her own speaking travel ($18K), light digital advertising ($15K), other ($11K)

**Pains:**
- AUM growth has flatlined for 3 quarters — first time in 8 years
- Her CCO has just made her remove the firm's AI chatbot widget over compliance concerns; she's frustrated and over-cautious
- A peer firm in Boston posts on LinkedIn constantly; their AUM is up 12% YoY
- Younger advisors at the firm want to "do AI things" but she doesn't know what's compliant

**Triggers:**
- A current client says they "almost moved their assets" to a firm that ChatGPT recommended
- A regulatory peer-share session mentions GEO/AEO is now common in advisory marketing
- A FPA RI event includes a session on AI search

**Decision criteria:**
- Compliance posture: every claim, every output, must be reviewable and archivable
- Conservative aesthetic — she does not want anything that looks "techy" or "young"
- References — must show similar-tier RIA clients (or compatible: law/CPA work)
- Cost: $30K–$60K/yr range; she will not exceed without a clear AUM-growth tie

**Objections:**
- *"My CCO will block this"* → Build the audit deliverable around CCO-friendliness; offer to attend the compliance review meeting
- *"Our content has SEC restrictions"* → Acknowledge real constraints; show case study of compliance-friendly content
- *"Will AI 'recommend us'? That sounds like solicitation"* → No — we optimize so AI *cites our published content* when users ask questions; we don't pay for placement

**Matched service:** **Audit ($1,500) → Foundation ($3,500) → GEO Growth retainer ($2,500/mo) with CCO-friendly content workflow.**
**Annual value:** $35,000.
**Sales cycle:** 10–14 weeks (compliance gate adds 4+ weeks).
**Sales notes:** First meeting at her office, in person. Bring a SEC-friendly content sample. Prepare to attend her CCO review. Patience required — when she signs, she stays.

**Triggers:**

**Annual value:** $39,000.
**Sales cycle:** 5–9 weeks.

## 4. Anti-ICP — Who NOT to Sell To

These prospects will waste cycles. Polite "not a fit" is the right answer:

- **Solo practitioner with sub-$500K revenue.** Budget too thin; needs the audit but won't buy retainer.
- **Pre-revenue startup.** Wrong stage; their content is too thin to optimize meaningfully.
- **B2C local services** (dentist, real estate broker, hair salon). Different buying psychology, different content economics. Refer to local SEO-focused agency.
- **Multi-state national chain** with corporate marketing. The local franchisee can't actually buy this.
- **Buyers who say "make us go viral"** or talk about "growth hacking." Wrong category — they want performance marketing, not GEO.
- **Compliance-paralyzed advisor without budget control.** If even Persona D's flexibility isn't there, walk away.

## 5. Buyer Insights for Sales Scripts

Patterns across all 5 personas worth surfacing:

1. **The "ChatGPT didn't recommend us" moment is the most reliable trigger.** Every persona has a version of this. Sales scripts should ask early: *"Have you typed your firm name into ChatGPT lately? What did you see?"*
2. **All five are budget-cautious but not budget-blocked.** They have spend; they need permission to redirect.
3. **All five fear looking foolish.** Risk reversal is critical — productized scope, fixed deliverables, exit-clause retainers.
4. **All five buy from people who *get* their world.** Vertical-specific content, peer references, and language fluency in their domain (CLE, GAAP, framework, AUM, RFP) is non-negotiable.
5. **All five are influenceable by peer behavior.** Case studies of named-vertical peer firms (even anonymized) are the single most persuasive sales asset.

## 6. Implications for the Website (Phase 3)

The ICP profiles directly drive website content:

- **Each persona gets a dedicated industry page** (5 pages total)
- **Each industry page features a persona-specific FAQ** (10 entries) addressing their *exact* objections from §2 above
- **Every CTA is "Free GEO Audit"** — the audit is the universal entry product across all 5 personas
- **Case studies are framed by industry**, not by year or by company size
- **Compliance language is included on the financial advisor page** (matches Jen's needs)
- **Visual quality must be high** (matches Tom's needs)
- **The home page hero must be specific enough** that each persona reads it and thinks "this is for me"
